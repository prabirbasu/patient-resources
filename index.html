Penile implants & reconstructive andrology

When tablets and injections stop working, a penile prosthesis is the one treatment that reliably restores the ability to have an erection whenever you want one, for as long as you want it.

The most common device is a three-piece inflatable implant: two cylinders inside the penis, a small pump in the scrotum, and a fluid reservoir in the lower abdomen. Nothing is visible from outside. A simpler malleable (bendable) implant suits men who want fewer parts and a shorter operation.

Reconstruction covers the rest of what goes wrong structurally: Peyronie's disease (curvature, from plication and grafting to combined implant surgery), urethral stricture (urethroplasty rather than repeated dilatation), and penile fracture or trauma repair.

Who typically benefits

  • ED that no longer responds to PDE5 tablets or intracavernosal injections
  • Diabetes-related ED, where the vascular and nerve damage is usually permanent
  • ED after prostate surgery, once recovery has plateaued
  • Peyronie's disease with curvature and weak erections, where one operation addresses both

Erectile dysfunction as a vascular sentinel event

The arteries of the penis are narrower than the arteries of the heart. They clog first — so ED is often the earliest visible sign of cardiovascular disease, typically two to five years ahead of a cardiac event.

That reframes the first consultation. Before we discuss tablets, we look at blood pressure, lipids, blood sugar, waist circumference and sleep. Treating ED well means treating the man, not just the erection.

Where medicine is the right answer, it is used properly: dosing, timing, and honest expectations. Where it is not, we move up the ladder — vacuum devices, intracavernosal injections, low-intensity shockwave therapy in selected cases, and implants when nothing else holds.

Commonly addressed

  • New-onset ED in men under 50, treated as a cardiovascular screening opportunity
  • Unconsummated marriage and performance anxiety, managed alongside the physical work-up
  • Pornography-induced ED and premature ejaculation, with a structured, evidence-based plan
  • Testosterone deficiency, diagnosed on proper morning samples before any replacement is considered

Male infertility

Half of all couples struggling to conceive have a male factor, yet the man is often the last to be examined. A proper andrological evaluation frequently changes the plan — and sometimes avoids IVF altogether.

Work-up goes beyond a single semen analysis: a repeat sample, hormones, scrotal examination and ultrasound for varicocele, and where indicated a sperm DNA fragmentation index (DFI), which conventional semen analysis does not capture.

Surgical options are used when the evidence supports them: microsurgical varicocelectomy for clinical varicocele with abnormal parameters, and micro-TESE sperm retrieval for non-obstructive azoospermia.

Commonly addressed

  • Varicocele — when to operate and when to leave it alone
  • High DNA fragmentation with recurrent pregnancy loss or failed ICSI
  • Azoospermia, obstructive and non-obstructive
  • Male accessory gland infection (MAGI) and its effect on semen quality

Functional & neuro-urology

Bladder problems are not an inevitable part of ageing. Urgency, leakage, incomplete emptying and night-time frequency all have causes that can be measured — and most of them can be treated.

The key investigation is urodynamics: a pressure-flow study that shows what the bladder and its outlet are actually doing, rather than guessing from symptoms alone. It is essential after spinal injury, in multiple sclerosis, in diabetic bladder, and before any surgery for incontinence.

Treatment ranges from pelvic floor training and medication to botulinum toxin injections into the bladder, sacral neuromodulation, and the artificial urinary sphincter for stress incontinence after prostate surgery.

Commonly addressed

  • Overactive bladder that has not responded to first-line medication
  • Neurogenic bladder after spinal cord injury or in neurological disease
  • Post-prostatectomy incontinence
  • Chronic pelvic pain and pelvic floor dysfunction in men
